Rugged Tablet and Terminal FAQ

The questions that decide whether a project is buildable: order size, timing, sealing, certification and what happens after the warranty. Each answer is the short version; the linked page carries the detail.

Quick answer: minimum order is 2 units, a sample of an agreed configuration is built in about 3 days, mass production runs about 25 days from sample approval, and the standard warranty is 12 months on manufacturing defects, extendable to 36 months on volume orders. Everything else depends on the model and the destination market.

Ordering and commercial terms

What is the minimum order quantity?

Two units on a standard build. One goes to the field trial, the other to application and device-management testing, so the pilot and the rollout do not queue behind the same device. A build with a new housing carries tooling and is quoted separately. More on the process in the order questions article.

How long does a sample take, and how long is production?

A sample of an agreed standard configuration is built in about three days, plus shipping. Mass production runs about 25 days from sample approval for the configuration and quantity on the order. Certification and tooling run on their own clocks and are scheduled before the sample is built.

What does the warranty cover?

Twelve months on manufacturing defects as standard, extendable to 36 months on volume orders. The scope is written into the quotation and the warranty card ships with the unit, because the boundary between a defect and damage depends on how the device was used.

Who handles shipping and paperwork?

Shipments leave Shenzhen under the incoterms agreed with the order, and collection by your own forwarder is a normal arrangement. The forwarder address, consignee details and the document set for your market are agreed before packing.

Can the hardware be customised?

Yes, on two routes. OEM reconfigures a platform already in production — module set, branding, packaging, firmware image. ODM takes a new device through mechanical design, tooling and mass production. Interfaces, housing, Android image and packaging are all in scope; see OEM and ODM.

Hardware and platform

Which Android versions are available?

Android 12 and Android 13 depending on the model, and the XH-700 also offers an Ubuntu build. The version matters less than the commitment behind it: ask in writing how often security patches are issued and for how many years the build stays current. See the Android platform note.

Is Google Mobile Services included?

GMS is certified on the models whose specification lists it. Where it is not listed, treat it as absent until confirmed: an application that depends on Google Play services needs that check before the order, not at the pilot.

Do you make a 5G tablet?

Yes, the XH-700 is the 5G model in the range. The other tablets carry 4G LTE. If 5G is a requirement, say so in the first message because it narrows the choice to one platform.

Which model is best for cold stores or wash-down areas?

The XH-800 is the 8-inch IP68 build rated −20 to +60 °C. Sealing and temperature are listed per model rather than per range, so compare the number on the specification page with your aisle temperature. The cold storage notes cover the failure modes.

Data capture

Which barcode engines can be fitted?

The range uses Honeywell, Zebra or Newland engines depending on the model and the symbologies in scope; the XH-800 lists Newland N1, Newland N2596 and Zebra 4100. Name the labels you scan and the engine is matched to them.

Do you offer fingerprint capture?

Yes. Capacitive modules are standard on the biometric terminals, and optional on tablets, including an FAP20 module on the XH-800 and XH-700. FBI PIV-071006 and Appendix F certification applies to the FAP20 and FAP30 modules, and Ministry of Public Security certification to the capacitive module; confirm the sensor, SDK and output format for the application.

Can the tablet read UHF RFID tags?

On the XH-800, yes: LF 125/134 kHz, HF 13.56 MHz and UHF are separate optional modules. On the other tablets, NFC 13.56 MHz is standard but LF/HF/UHF is not in the published options. Read range depends on tag, orientation, metal and liquid, not on the module alone — see the UHF note.

Can it read national ID cards?

The offline ID card reader is an optional module on several models, including the XH-800 and XH-700. The document types and the software compatibility have to be confirmed at order stage, because the module alone does not decide whether an application can use it.

Sealing, temperature and drops

What do IP65, IP67 and IP68 mean in practice?

The first digit is dust and the second is water. IP65 survives water jets, IP67 a temporary immersion, IP68 continuous immersion at a stated depth. Which one a project needs depends on the exposure, not on the price — the IP rating guide has the detail.

Does a rugged tablet survive a drop onto concrete?

Most models list a 1.2 m drop onto a wooden floor with the device powered on. That is a published test condition, not a guarantee for every surface: concrete is harder than wood, and the drop rating does not cover a device that lands on a corner with an accessory attached.

Is MIL-STD-810H certification included?

MIL-STD-810H is a test method standard, not a certificate, and no body issues one. Ask for the test report by name if a tender requires it, and check which methods were run — the explainer covers what each method means.

Certification and compliance

Which approvals come with the standard build?

GMS on the models whose specification lists it, FBI PIV-071006 and Appendix F certification on the FAP20 and FAP30 fingerprint modules, Ministry of Public Security certification on the capacitive module, and the IP rating the model is tested to. Radio approvals for a market — CE, UKCA, FCC or local type approval — are scoped per project and quoted before production. See the certification guide.

Can you certify a device for my market?

Yes, and it is cheaper to decide before tooling than after the first shipment, because a retrofit means retesting the device. The test plan follows the market you ship to; certification support covers scheduling.

Software, fleet and support

Is there an SDK?

API and SDK support is provided for the modules fitted to the build — scan engine, fingerprint reader, RFID radio, GNSS. Output formats such as NMEA or EPC are confirmed before the order rather than assumed.

How is a fleet managed?

The Android builds work with the usual Android Enterprise tooling. Confirm the specific MDM agent against the exact OS build: a device on a recent Android version with no patch commitment is a worse purchase than one version behind with a documented policy. See fleet management.

What happens when a unit fails in year two?

Every unit is serialised and traceable to its production record, so a failure can be traced to the batch, the test data and the module lot. Repairs, spare parts and the warranty scope are listed separately in the quotation, so the cost of keeping a fleet running after the warranty ends is visible before the order.

Still unanswered?

Send the workflow, the environment, the modules, the quantity, the destination market and the date units are needed. That is enough for a configuration and a lead time rather than another question.
