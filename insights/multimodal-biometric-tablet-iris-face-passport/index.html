Multimodal Biometric Tablet: Iris, Face, Print and Passport

Five capture routes · Fusion policy · Fallback chains · Anti-spoofing · What to specify

Home / News / Multimodal Capture

By Xinhong Smart Engineering Team · Published 24 November 2026 · 10 min read

Quick answer: a multimodal device is not one with more sensors, it is one with a written policy for which modality is primary, which is the fallback, what happens when two of them disagree, and what the record keeps. The XH-800 can carry all five routes in one 8-inch IP68 enclosure - iris, fingerprint, face with liveness, an e-passport reader and 13.56 MHz card reading - but without that policy you get a slower counter and an evidence trail nobody can defend. The policy is the product; the modules are the parts.

The five routes, and what each is actually for

Every modality answers a different question, and the differences decide where each one belongs in the chain.

RouteAnswersFails whenIn the range
FingerprintIs this the enrolled person, on the deviceRidges worn, wet, dirty, bandaged; small platens on off-centre placementFAP10, FAP20 and FAP30 options across the handhelds and tablets
FaceIs a live person present, and is it this personHarsh sunlight, masks, extreme angles; without liveness, a photographTablet models with binocular or mono liveness, 0.2–1.5 m working distance
IrisA stable biometric with no contactGlasses reflections, unsteady hold, a dirty or scratched windowOptional on the XH-800 and XH-500; on the XH-800 it sits alongside the other four routes
Passport and e-IDIs the document genuine, and does it belong to this personWorn MRZ, damaged chip, wrong access keys, an unsupported document typee-passport and national e-ID chip reading on the XH-800 and XH-400; document camera and full counter approach on the dual-screen terminal
Bank and ID cardsIs the credential this person holdsContactless only in the standard build; contact chips need a different readerNFC at 13.56 MHz on the XH-800 covers ISO/IEC 14443 A/B, 15693, 18092 and MIFARE, which includes contactless bank cards; a contact chip reader is a separate module

Note the shape of that table. The document route is the only one that verifies something outside the person, and the card route is the only one that verifies a credential issued by a third party. The three biometric routes all answer variants of "is this the right person", and stacking all three does not add three times the assurance - it adds three failure paths and one policy problem.

What multimodal actually buys you

Four things, in order of how often they justify the cost.

Population coverage

Single-modality deployments fail on a predictable slice of the population: worn fingerprints in manual work, glasses and head coverings for face, unsteady hold for iris. A second route converts those failures into ordinary transactions. This, not security, is the reason most multimodal programmes exist.

A fallback that is written down

When the primary route fails, the counter has to do something. Without a documented fallback the operator improvises - a manual entry, a photo of a document, a phone call - and the improvisation is what an auditor finds two years later. A second modality turns an exception into a recorded event with its own code.

Presentation-attack resistance

A fingerprint sensor that returns a match on a lifted print, a camera that accepts a printed photograph and a document reader that trusts a photocopy are three different failures. Combining routes raises the cost of an attack because the attacker has to defeat the specific pair, and because the disagreement itself is a signal worth logging. Liveness detection belongs here: the face route is only a security control when it rejects a photograph or a mask.

Evidence in one record

The value of capturing five things is that they attach to one transaction with one timestamp and one operator ID. That is what makes a record defensible: not that five biometrics were taken, but that they were taken together, on one device, and stored in a form the registry accepts.

The part nobody specifies: the fusion policy

This is where multimodal projects fail quietly. The modules work, the demo works, and then the first disagreement between two modalities has no rule.

DecisionWhat has to be written, not assumed
Primary routeWhich modality is attempted first, and why that one for this population
Fallback orderThe sequence after a failure, how many attempts, and when a supervisor is called
Disagreement ruleFingerprint matches and face does not: accept, reject, or escalate - and what the record says
Matching mode1:1 against a claimed identity, 1:N against a local gallery, or both, and which threshold applies to each
Offline behaviourWhat is stored on the device, for how long, encrypted how, and what is discarded after sync
Record contentWhich templates or images are kept, in which format, and which are never written to disk

Two of those rows decide most of the cost. Matching mode determines whether the device needs a local gallery and how much storage and battery that costs; record content determines whether the build is a biometric capture device or a biometric database, which is a legal question in most markets, not only a technical one.

The bank card question

This is the requirement that most often arrives without its detail, and the detail changes the hardware.

Contactless bank cards are covered by the standard NFC reader: 13.56 MHz, ISO/IEC 14443 Type A/B, at a couple of centimetres. That covers tapping a card, a phone or a transit credential.

Contact chip cards - what a domestic payment scheme or a bank branch typically asks for - need a physical reader with a card slot and, usually, a SAM for the keys. That is a different module set and a different build, and it has to be named at the specification stage. Magnetic stripe is a third case again, and one that most new programmes are trying to leave behind.

If the programme names "bank card" without saying which, ask which one. The answer decides whether the device needs a slot in the housing, which is also a sealing question.

Five routes on one enclosure: what it costs

Each module takes volume, power and a hole in the housing, and the holes are where water gets in. Adding iris to a tablet means an optical window in the front bezel, in the place a dropped device lands. Adding a contact card reader means a slot with a sealed flap. Adding a passport reader means either an antenna around the document window or a hand-held pass over the MRZ.

The XH-800 is the platform in our range where that is resolved: an 8-inch IP68 enclosure with room for an iris window, a fingerprint platen, a front camera with binocular liveness, a document reader and the NFC antenna, on a 10,000 mAh battery. It works because the device is carried in a holster rather than held all day, and because the sealing was designed around the windows rather than added after them. Where the deployment is a handheld in continuous use, the practical combination drops to two biometrics plus a credential - fingerprint, face and NFC - with an explicit statement of which route the field team falls back to.

What to put in the RFQ

  1. The primary route and the fallback order, per population, not per device.
  2. The standards: fingerprint record format and FAP profile, document type and access keys, card technology (contactless, contact, stripe).
  3. Matching mode and thresholds, and whether a local gallery is required.
  4. The disagreement rule and what the operator is allowed to do without a supervisor.
  5. Offline duration and the storage plan for templates that accumulate before sync.
  6. The record format the registry accepts, agreed before the build, because it decides whether the SDK can produce the output at all.
  7. The sealing and drop conditions, given the windows and slots the modules require.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can one tablet capture iris, fingerprint, face, passport and bank cards?
Yes, on the XH-800. It is an 8-inch IP68 platform that can be configured with iris recognition, a capacitive or optical fingerprint module, a front camera with binocular liveness, an e-passport and national e-ID chip reader, and 13.56 MHz NFC for contactless cards. The build is configured to order rather than stocked, because the module set, the windows and the sealing are settled together. The one requirement that changes the hardware is a contact chip card reader, which needs a card slot.
Which modality should be primary?
Whatever your population succeeds with most often. In manual and industrial work that is usually fingerprint despite the worn ridges, because it is fast and needs no cooperation. In elderly or assisted populations it is often the document plus face, because fingers are unreliable and the card is already present. Choose by measurement on a sample of your own users, not by the security ranking of the modalities.
Is multimodal more secure than single-modality?
Only if the routes are independent and the disagreement is treated as a signal. Three sensors that can each be spoofed with a printed photograph are not more secure than one good sensor with liveness. The security comes from the policy - which combination is required, what happens on disagreement, and whether the attempt is logged - rather than from the count of modules.
Does the bank card reader need a SAM?
For contact chip cards on payment schemes, usually yes: the SAM holds the keys and the transaction logic. For contactless cards read as an identifier rather than as a payment, the standard NFC reader is enough. The difference matters at the specification stage, because a SAM slot is a mechanical feature of the housing, not a software option.
How long does a five-modality enrolment take?
Longer than the sum of the individual captures, because each route adds a failure path and a retry decision. The document step is usually the slowest and the one that fails most, since it depends on the condition of a card the operator does not control. Time a pilot on your own population before committing to a throughput target; a vendor number measured on cooperative volunteers will not transfer.
What should be stored on the device?
As little as the workflow allows: templates rather than images where the registry permits, encrypted at rest, deleted after a verified sync, and never written to removable storage. Many jurisdictions treat a stored biometric template as personal data with retention limits, so the storage plan belongs in the legal review, not only in the technical one.
