Mobile Voter Registration with a Biometric Tablet

A registration desk in a school hall has one working socket, no wired network, and a queue that started forming before the team unlocked the door. What the tablet does at hour six, on its own battery, with dust on the screen, matters more than what it does in a showroom.

The site decides the specification

Enrolment teams go where people already are: community halls, school rooms, temporary shelters. Three conditions at those sites shape the hardware more than any feature list does.

Power is uncertain. A mains connection may exist, may be shared, or may not be there at all. The device has to finish a shift on internal battery, and the number that matters is capacity together with what the modules draw while capturing.

Light is not controlled. Capturing a fingerprint beside an open doorway in bright daylight is a different problem from capturing one under office lighting. A 10.1-inch IPS panel at 1200 × 1920 with 450 nit brightness stays readable in that setting, and the screen is large enough to show the enrolment form and the capture preview at the same time.

The device is carried, not mounted. At 292 × 171.5 × 20 mm and 1.1 kg, an operator can hold it for a registration, set it down, and pick it up again all day. The listed ratings cover a 1.2 m drop onto a wooden floor, operation from −20 °C to +60 °C, and 95% non-condensing humidity.

What one enrolment station actually does

Set the paperwork aside and a registration station performs four jobs. Each one maps to a hardware decision, and each one is where a specification usually goes wrong.

Read the identity document

If the roll is built on chip-based ID cards, the standard 13.56 MHz NFC reader handles ISO/IEC 14443 A/B, 15693, 18092 and MIFARE protocols at a listed reading distance of 2–4 cm. Paper documents without a chip need the optional ID card recognition module instead, and documents carrying a printed barcode need the optional Newland or Zebra scan engine. Confirm which document types your roll actually uses before choosing modules — that single answer removes most of the optional hardware from the list.

Capture the person

The optional fingerprint module is the part that decides whether the record is still usable a year later. It is offered as capacitive or optical, and the choice depends on the fingers you expect: dry or worn ridges behave differently from dirty or calloused ones, and a module that performs well in an office can struggle with hands that have spent years handling soil and fibre. Our comparison of FAP 10, FAP 20 and FAP 30 covers the capture area and image size behind each level. Where the workflow calls for face or iris instead, the optional binocular liveness or iris camera modules take that role.

Check whether the person is already on the roll

This is the job buyers most often assign to the wrong place. Deduplication is a matching problem against a central register: one template compared against millions, at a threshold your authority sets. That runs on the backend. The tablet’s job is to capture cleanly, package the record, transmit it, and show the operator the answer.

Hold the record until the network returns

Coverage at remote sites is intermittent, so records have to queue on the device and sync when a connection appears. The XH-500 carries 4G LTE across FDD-LTE bands 1/2/3/4/5/7/8/12/17/19/20/26/28a/28b and TDD-LTE bands 34/38/39/40/41, plus WCDMA bands 1/2/4/5/8, alongside dual-band 802.11 a/b/g/n/ac WiFi and Bluetooth 5.0. Local buffering sits on 64 GB of internal storage, with 6 GB + 128 GB and 8 GB + 128 GB optional, and a TF slot accepting cards up to 512 GB.

Teams that walk a catchment rather than staff a fixed desk often pair it with the 5-inch XH-100 handheld for door-to-door work and keep the tablet at the station. Both run the same capture stack, so records from either device land in one queue.

Choosing the fingerprint module

Two questions settle it, and both have to be answered by your software team rather than read off a hardware catalogue.

First: what capture profile does the register require? FAP 10, FAP 20 and FAP 30 differ in platen area and image dimensions, and a mismatch shows up as rejected records months after deployment, not during evaluation.

Second: what output format does the backend expect? ISO/IEC 19794-2 templates and 19794-4 images are the usual answers, but authorities running their own matcher sometimes specify a vendor format. Pick the sensor after that answer, not before.

Capacitive and optical modules both stay on the option list for a reason. Capacitive sensors are compact and common for single-finger verification; optical sensors handle a wider range of skin condition and are often chosen for enrolment, where ridge quality varies across the population. If your pilot group includes manual labourers, test both.

What the tablet does not decide

Where templates are stored, how they are encrypted, who holds the keys and what the audit trail records are system decisions made by the deploying authority and its integration partner. The device supplies the sensor, the SDK and the transport. Those protections are implemented by the configured system, so confirm storage, encryption and offline behaviour with your integration team before rollout.

A checklist before you request a quote

1. Document types. Chip card, paper certificate, barcode, or a mix. This sets the NFC, recognition and scan-engine options.

2. Fingerprint profile and output format. The FAP level plus ISO/IEC 19794-2 or -4, or a specified vendor format.

3. Carrier bands. Match the LTE bands listed above against the operators in your region. Devices built for one market do not always attach in another.

4. Volume per device per day. This drives storage sizing, battery planning and how many spare units a district needs.

5. Quantity and delivery window. Both affect module availability and lead time.

6. Customisation. Pre-installed applications, boot logo, PSAM slots, connector layout and housing colour are handled through our custom build process.

Questions we get asked

Can the tablet deduplicate without a network?
No. Matching runs against the central register on your backend. The device captures, queues locally and syncs when a connection is available, then displays the result to the operator.
Which fingerprint module should we order?
Whichever one meets the capture profile and output format your backend requires. Send us the FAP level and the expected format, and confirm the sensor and SDK at order stage.
Will it read our ID cards?
The standard NFC reader supports ISO/IEC 14443 A/B, 15693, 18092 and MIFARE at 2–4 cm. Card behaviour varies by issuer, so send samples for testing before a full order.
Is it waterproof?
The listed ratings cover a 1.2 m drop onto a wooden floor, −20 °C to +60 °C operation and 95% non-condensing humidity. State any ingress protection requirement your deployment has, and we will confirm what the housing supports before you order.

Planning an enrolment rollout? Send us your document types, fingerprint output format, daily volume per device and delivery window. We will confirm module configuration, MOQ and lead time, and flag the parts that usually need adjusting before deployment.
