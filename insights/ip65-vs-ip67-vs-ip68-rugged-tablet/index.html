IP65 vs IP67 vs IP68 vs IP69K: Rugged Tablet Protection Ratings Explained

IEC 60529 · IK Impact Code · What IP Does NOT Cover · How to Verify Vendor Claims

The IP code is the most quoted — and most misunderstood — specification on any rugged tablet datasheet. This guide explains what each digit means, compares IP65/IP67/IP68/IP69K, and covers the four things IP ratings do not protect against. It complements our Industrial Tablet Buying Guide 2026.

Anatomy of the IP Code (IEC 60529)

An IP code has two digits. The first describes protection against solid objects; the second against liquids. A higher number is not universally "better" — each describes a specific test.

First Digit — Solids

DigitMeaningDetail
5Dust-protectedLimited ingress permitted; not fully sealed
6Dust-tightNo ingress after 8 hours in a dust chamber; vacuum test

For industrial duty always require first digit 6. Digit 5 allows enough ingress to cause long-term failures with metal or mineral dust.

Second Digit — Liquids

DigitTest conditionPractical translation
56.3 mm nozzle, 12.5 L/min, 3 min from 3 mLow-pressure jets — cleaning spray
612.5 mm nozzle, 100 L/min, 3 min from 3 mPowerful jets — heavy washdown
7Immersion 1 m depth, 30 minRain, puddles, accidental submersion
8Continuous immersion, manufacturer specifies depth & durationVaries — ask specifics
9KSteam jet 80 °C, 80–100 bar, close rangeHigh-pressure hot washdown

The trap: "IP68" without stated conditions is marketing. Ask: Depth? Duration? Fresh or salt water? Tested by whom?

IK Impact Rating — The Missing Half

IP says nothing about hitting things. For that you need the IK code (IEC 62262), measured in impact joules:

RatingImpact energyReal-world equivalent
IK061 JLight knocks
IK072 JDrop of a small tool
IK085 JRecommended industrial minimum
IK09 / IK1010 J / 20 JVandal-resistant, heavy equipment

Ask for IK07 minimum, IK08 preferred. Pair it with MIL-STD-810H drop certification, which simulates actual falls rather than pendulum impacts — both are explained in our drop resistance section.

What IP Ratings Do NOT Cover

This is where most buying mistakes happen. A dust-tight, immersion-proof tablet can still fail tomorrow if you ignore these:

  1. Temperature. IP ≠ cold or heat survival. Specify operating range separately (-10 °C to 60 °C standard; -20 °C/-30 °C for cold chain).
  2. Chemicals. Oils, solvents and disinfectants attack gaskets and polycarbonate even when water does not. Request chemical compatibility data.
  3. Vibration. Vehicle-mounted units need MIL-STD-810H Method 514 vibration validation, independent of IP.
  4. Salt fog / corrosion. Marine and coastal deployments need salt-fog testing (IEC 60068-2-52), not just IP67.
  5. Altitude & pressure and EMC/EMI behavior in industrial RF environments.

How to Verify a Vendor's IP Claim

  1. Request the third-party laboratory report — SGS, TUV, Intertek or equivalent — not only a datasheet icon.
  2. Verify the standard cited is IEC 60529 and check the report date.
  3. Confirm the tested unit matches your purchased configuration (same ports, covers, battery door, dock).
  4. Ask whether accessories were included in testing — docks and sleds change the sealing path.
  5. Get specific IP68 conditions written into the purchase contract.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is IP68 automatically better than IP67?
Not always. IP67 guarantees 1 m immersion for 30 minutes under a fixed standard test. IP68 conditions are set by the manufacturer and may be shallower but longer, or deeper—there is no single meaning. Always ask for the exact depth, duration and water condition (fresh vs salt) in writing.
Can I use an IP67 tablet in heavy rain all day?
Yes. IP67 (immersion rated) comfortably exceeds driving rain. Continuous outdoor operation is fine; the real-world failure points are usually the USB/charging port cover (ensure it is seated) and repeated pressure-washing at close range, which requires IP69K.
What is IP69K and who needs it?
IP69K is protection against high-pressure, high-temperature steam jets (80 °C, 80–100 bar, close range). It is required for food processing washdown, pharmaceutical cleanrooms, and vehicles cleaned with pressure washers. It is not needed for typical warehouse or field work.
Does an IP rating cover screen cracking from drops?
No. Ingress protection says nothing about mechanical impact or drop resistance. Impact protection is rated separately with the IK code (IEC 62262), and drop survival is covered by MIL-STD-810H Method 516.6. A tablet can be IP67 and still crack when dropped.
My tablet is IP67 but the touchscreen failed in a freezer. Why?
Because IP does not cover temperature. Operating temperature range is a separate specification. In cold-chain work you need a model explicitly rated for -20 °C or -30 °C, ideally with a heated display element to prevent condensation and frost.
How do I verify a supplier IP claim is real?
Request the third-party test report (from SGS, TUV, Intertek or equivalent), not just a datasheet icon. Check: standard cited (IEC 60529), test lab name, report number, date, exact conditions, and that the tested sample matches your purchased configuration (ports, covers, accessories change sealing).
Do port covers and accessories affect the rating?
Yes. Ratings apply to the device as tested, typically with all covers seated. A missing port door, third-party battery door or unpadded dock can void effective protection. This is why Xinhong tests the complete accessory set for vehicle-dock configurations.
What is IK rating and which level should I ask for?
IK (IEC 62262) rates resistance to mechanical impact energy in joules, from IK00 to IK10. For industrial tablets ask for IK07 or higher (2 J); IK08 (5 J) is common on fully rugged units. It complements MIL-STD-810H drop testing, addressing repeated localized impacts rather than single falls.
Is IP65 enough for indoor warehouses?
Usually yes. IP65 handles airborne dust and low-pressure cleaning jets, which covers most indoor logistics. Choose IP67 when forklifts work outdoors in rain, floors are washed with high pressure, or the device may be dropped into standing water.
Does higher IP always mean higher cost?
Generally yes, because sealing complexity, gasket quality and validation add cost. However the step from IP65 to IP67 is usually modest (often 3–8% on unit price), whereas jumping to true IP69K-certified hardware is more significant because it also implies chemical-resistant materials.
