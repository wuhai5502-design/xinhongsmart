XH-600 Smart POS Terminal Buying Guide: How to Configure the Right Build

Magstripe · Contact IC · NFC · 58 mm printer · PCI PTS 5.x · EMV L1/L2 · PBOC 3.0 · Android 15

Home / News / XH-600 Smart POS Terminal Buying Guide

By Xinhong Smart Engineering Team · Published 16 October 2026

Quick answer: Specify the XH-600 against the payment flow and the destination market, not the headline spec: which card paths the acquirer actually settles, whether the receipt has to leave the same hand-held unit, the connectivity that survives the venue, a battery that covers the shift, and the certification package the market demands. Every one of those is a build decision made before the line starts — not a setting changed after delivery.

What a Smart POS Terminal Replaces

A smart POS terminal collapses a counter installation — terminal, PIN pad, receipt printer and the cabling between them — into one hand-held unit running Android. The XH-600 puts a 58 mm thermal printer in the same body as the card readers, so the payment and its receipt come out of one device.

The useful question is not whether integration is good in principle, but which items it removes from your bill of materials:

Traditional counterWith the XH-600What changes for you
Card terminalMagstripe, contact IC and contactless NFC integratedOne device to certify and one to support
Separate PIN padBuilt-in virtual PIN padNo pairing, no second battery, no second cable
Receipt printerBuilt-in high-speed 58 mm thermal printerNo dock, no printer cabling per lane
Register or tablet for the business appAndroid 15.0 with SDK accessPayment and business logic on one screen
Hand-held barcode scannerRear 5 MP camera with 1D / 2D decodingFewer items carried and charged per lane

Where the terminal is stationary and a till already exists, this consolidation matters less. Where the operator walks — table-side, curbside, delivery, a seasonal lane — it is usually the whole argument.

The Seven Decisions That Shape Your Build

Every XH-600 leaves the line configured. These are the seven choices that fix that configuration, in the order a procurement team usually meets them.

1. Which card paths the acquirer actually settles

Most buyers over-specify here. List the paths the acquirer will settle in your market and drop the rest.

PathXH-600 supportSpecify when
Magnetic stripeTracks 1 / 2 / 3, bi-directional swipingLegacy card bases, or as a fallback where chip read fails
Contact ICType A (5.0 V), Type B (3.0 V), Type C (1.8 V); EMV and PBOCAny EMV market — this is the default
Contactless NFCISO/IEC 14443 Type A and B, Mifare1; EMV and PBOC; card reading indicatorTap-to-pay, transit fare, loyalty and closed-loop cards

The card reading indicator is worth asking for specifically: it confirms a successful read on the device itself, which removes the most common source of double-swipes at a busy lane.

2. Whether the receipt has to leave the same unit

The built-in printer shares the terminal battery, so printing draws from the same budget as the screen and the radio. Decide the duty cycle before you choose the battery tier.

BatteryCapacitySpecify when
Standard2,800 mAh / 7.4 VStandard shift, printer used intermittently, charger reachable
Optional upgrade3,600 mAh / 7.4 VLong shifts, heavy printing, or a full day away from a charger

3. Connectivity and slot configuration

4G / 3G / 2G with hotspot, Bluetooth, and built-in GPS with AGPS cover mobile work. The slot count is the part buyers miss: SIM × 2, SAM × 2, SD × 1. Two SIM slots let the terminal carry the acquirer configuration and a fallback, and two SAM slots let it hold the security modules a scheme or an issuer requires. If your flow needs two SAMs, confirm it before the build — it is not a field change.

4. Cardholder verification beyond a PIN

The 500 DPI fingerprint module is optional, with a 15.24 × 20.32 mm active area, listed against STQC and FBI single-finger profiles FAP10, FAP20 and FAP30. Specify it where the payment or the identity flow requires capture; the terminal also supports electronic signature capture on the 5.5-inch capacitive screen where it does not.

Go deeper: The profile you name changes the sensor and the template format, so read our guide to FAP 10, FAP 20 and FAP 30 capture requirements before you commit. For counter-side issuance rather than payment, see bank card issuance with a biometric terminal.

5. The certification package for the destination market

This is the decision that most often moves a delivery date. The standard build carries a long list, but scheme approvals are regional and the test plan has to be scheduled.

6. OS, SDK and application integration

Android 15.0 with 4 GB RAM and 32 GB eMMC. Your application drives the card paths, the printer and the scanner through the SDK, and PIN-pad encryption covers DES, 3DES, RSA, SHA-256 and AES. Agree the API surface and the output formats before the order is placed — after that point they are build parameters, not settings.

7. Physical build, accessories and packaging

Housing colour, boot logo, pre-installed applications, packaging and charger plug are all decided before the line starts. The standard accessory set is the power adapter, user manual, warranty card, USB cable and one roll of 58 mm thermal paper; a barcode scanner charging base and a silicone protective case are optional.

Deployment Scenarios in Detail

Chain supermarkets and convenience stores

One unit takes the card and prints the receipt, so a lane does not need a terminal, a printer and the cabling between them. The 5.5-inch screen and 202.4 mm body sit in a hand through a full shift, and the magstripe path stays available as a fallback when a chip read fails.

Restaurants and bakeries

Table-side payment and a printed receipt from the same device, with 4G keeping the terminal connected when the venue WiFi is saturated. Choose the 3,600 mAh pack for a service that runs without a charging opportunity.

Delivery and mobile collection

4G / 3G / 2G with hotspot plus built-in GPS and AGPS, so the terminal works away from a fixed counter and each transaction carries its location. Where the flow needs proof of handover, this is the scenario that most often justifies the fingerprint module.

Queue-busting and seasonal lanes

Dual SIM and dual SAM slots let the terminal carry the acquirer configuration and a fallback, which keeps a temporary lane running when one path is unavailable. Charging bases and protective cases are packed to match the deployment plan.

Banking and card issuance counters

This is the build where the fingerprint module stops being optional. Capture happens on the same device that reads the card, so the customer does not move between two pieces of equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the XH-600 print receipts without a separate printer?
Yes. A high-speed 58 mm thermal printer is built into the body and shares the same 2,800 mAh 7.4 V battery as the rest of the terminal, so the receipt is printed from the unit the operator is already holding. A 3,600 mAh pack is available where a shift runs long enough to need it.
Which card types does it accept?
Magnetic stripe on tracks 1, 2 and 3 with bi-directional swiping, contact IC cards at 5.0 V, 3.0 V or 1.8 V, and contactless NFC covering ISO/IEC 14443 Type A and B plus Mifare1. The IC and contactless paths are built to EMV and PBOC, and a card reading indicator confirms a successful read.
Is the fingerprint reader included?
It is optional. The module captures at 500 DPI over a 15.24 × 20.32 mm active area and is listed against STQC and FBI single-finger profiles FAP10, FAP20 and FAP30. Specify it where the payment flow requires cardholder verification beyond a PIN; the terminal also supports signature capture on the touchscreen.
Can it run our own payment application?
The XH-600 runs Android 15.0, so your application drives the card paths, the printer and the scanner directly through the SDK. Encryption for the PIN pad covers DES, 3DES, RSA, SHA-256 and AES. Agree the API surface and output formats before the order is placed.
Can the battery be upgraded for long shifts?
Yes. The standard build carries a 2,800 mAh 7.4 V lithium battery and a 3,600 mAh 7.4 V pack is available as an option. Choose the larger pack where a shift runs long, where the printer is used heavily, or where the terminal works away from a charger for the whole day.
Which certifications ship with the standard build?
The standard build is listed against PCI PTS 5.x, EMV Contact Terminal L1 and L2, EMV Contactless Terminal L1, Paywave, Paypass, PBOC 3.0 L1 and L2, QUICS and the UnionPay approvals, plus 3C, FCC and CE. Market-specific approvals are agreed before production so the test plan fits the schedule.
What are the MOQ, sample and lead times?
MOQ is 2 units. A sample of the agreed configuration is built and shipped in about 3 days, and mass production runs about 25 days from sample approval. The schedule for your configuration and quantity is confirmed in writing with the quotation.
