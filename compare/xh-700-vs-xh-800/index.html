XH-700 vs XH-800: Which Rugged Tablet Fits

Two current tablets, two different jobs. The XH-700 is a 10.1-inch 5G model with the faster platform and memory up to 16 GB + 256 GB. The XH-800 is an 8-inch IP68 build rated down to −20 °C with an optional RFID module. Choose by screen size and environment first, platform second.

Quick answer: pick the XH-700 when the work needs a large screen, 5G and an Ubuntu option; pick the XH-800 when the device has to be held in one hand, survive a wet or freezing environment, or read UHF RFID tags. Both run Android 13, both carry 10000 mAh, and both are quoted at a 2-unit minimum.

Pick the XH-700 if

The screen does the work. WMS pick lists, MES screens, vehicle dashboards and counter applications all want the 1200 × 1920 panel. At 10.1 inches you read a table of rows rather than three lines of text.

You need 5G or Ubuntu. The XH-700 carries 5G SA/NSA and offers an Ubuntu build next to Android 13, which is the only one of the two that leaves the Android toolchain. If the application is a Linux kiosk or an edge inference box, that decides it before anything else.

The workload is compute-bound. The UIS7885 with its 8 TOPS NPU is a different class from the MTK8788, and the 16 GB + 256 GB tier exists for the applications that need it.

Pick the XH-800 if

It is held in one hand. 246 × 148 mm fits a hand and a dash mount where a 10.1-inch tablet does not; 750 g is the cost of the smaller body and the thicker housing.

The environment is wet or freezing. IP68 is continuous immersion rather than splash, and the operating floor is −20 °C instead of −10 °C. In a cold store or on a wash-down line that is the difference that matters.

The tags are already fitted. LF, HF and UHF are separate optional modules on the XH-800. If the project reads pallet or asset tags, this is the model in the range that can do it.

What they share

Both run Android 13 on a 10000 mAh battery, both are quoted at a 2-unit minimum with a sample in about three days, and both are configured to order rather than picked from stock. The cost-of-ownership note applies to either: the purchase price is the minority of a three-year bill.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for warehouse picking?
Neither, if the picking is one-handed at height: that job belongs to a handheld PDA. For receiving, put-away and supervision the XH-700's 10.1-inch screen is the better fit; for narrow aisles where the device is carried rather than mounted, the XH-800 is the one that fits a hand.
Can the XH-800 run 5G?
No. The XH-800 is a 4G LTE build. 5G is on the XH-700, with SA/NSA support across N1, N3, N5, N8, N28, N41, N78 and N79 in the global build.
Does the XH-700 have an RFID module?
Not in the published options. The XH-700 lists a 2D imager, FAP20 fingerprint, an ID card reader and a macro camera; NFC 13.56 MHz is standard on both models. If the project needs LF, HF or UHF, the XH-800 is the model to specify.
Is IP68 worth paying for over IP67?
Only if the device is immersed rather than splashed or rained on. IP67 survives a temporary immersion test; IP68 is rated for continuous immersion at a stated depth. Wash-down areas, fish processing and some food plants need the second; a warehouse floor does not.
Do both support the same MDM platforms?
Both are Android 13 devices, so the usual Android Enterprise tooling applies. What differs is the validation: confirm the specific MDM agent and the OS build together, because an Ubuntu build is managed differently from an Android image and the two are not interchangeable.
What is the minimum order for either model?
Two units, the same as the rest of the range: one for a field trial, one for application and device-management testing. A sample of an agreed configuration is built in about three days and production runs about 25 days from sample approval.
