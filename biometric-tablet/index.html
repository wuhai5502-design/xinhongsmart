WHAT IT IS

A tablet that captures identity, not just data

A biometric tablet combines an industrial Android tablet with an identity capture module mounted in the housing and wired to the mainboard. Instead of scanning a document and typing the result into a separate computer, the operator captures the fingerprint, reads the identity card or taps an NFC credential, and the record is created on the same device — with the timestamp, the operator ID and the transaction attached.

Because the module is part of the build rather than an accessory plugged into a port, the device stays sealed, the capture position is repeatable, and the capture module survives the same drops, dust and water spray as the tablet around it. That is the difference between a consumer tablet with a dongle and a biometric terminal that can be deployed for years.

CAPTURE ROUTES

Six ways to capture identity

Most deployments use two or three of these. Which ones you need is settled by the credential your population carries and the record you have to produce — not by the device.

Fingerprint

Single-finger capture with capacitive or optical sensors, selected by the profile your application has to produce.

  • FAP10 capacitive sensor — compact, low power
  • FAP20 and FAP30 optical sensors (Slim2 / Slim3)
  • Record format and SDK output confirmed before ordering
  • FAP 10, FAP 20 and FAP 30 compared

Face recognition

Capture without contact, with liveness detection to reject a photograph or a mask.

  • Face recognition with liveness detection on tablet models
  • 0.2–1.5 m working distance, ≤1 s recognition
  • ≥99.72% verification accuracy
  • Available on the XH-300 10.1-inch industrial tablet

NFC and contactless smart card

Read the chip instead of typing from the card, at 13.56 MHz.

  • NFC ST21NFC at 13.56 MHz
  • ISO/IEC 14443 Type A/B, ISO/IEC 15693, 18092 and MIFARE
  • Listed reading distance 2–4 cm
  • Contactless bank cards, transit cards, chip ID cards, access badges

ID card

Read the national identity card offline, with hardware decoding rather than software guesswork.

  • Second-generation Chinese ID card reader, works offline
  • Ministry of Public Security decoder module, hardware decoding
  • Online version available where the workflow depends on a central service

Passport and travel documents

Two independent checks on the same document — optical and chip.

  • Optical MRZ reading of the two or three-line machine-readable zone
  • NFC chip reading following ICAO 9303, with BAC and PACE on newer documents
  • MRZ stays available as a fallback for documents that carry no chip
  • How document authentication works

Barcode and documents

Pair identity capture with the document or label that has to travel with it.

  • Rear 2D engines from Newland, Honeywell N3680 or Zebra 4710
  • A3 document camera scanners for service counters
  • Dual-screen terminals so the applicant sees what the operator captures

FAQ

Biometric tablet questions

What is a biometric tablet?
A biometric tablet is a rugged Android tablet or handheld terminal with a capture module connected to the mainboard — a fingerprint sensor, an ID card reader or an NFC reader — so identity can be captured and verified on the device itself, at the point of service, without a separate PC.
Which fingerprint profiles are available — FAP 10, FAP 20 or FAP 30?
FAP10 capacitive and FAP20 or FAP30 optical sensors are available depending on the model. The profile determines the capture area and image dimensions, which in turn decide the record format your application can produce. Our FAP 10, FAP 20 and FAP 30 guide explains the differences and what to confirm before ordering.
Does identity verification work offline?
The second-generation ID card reader works offline and an online version is also available. Capture, matching and storage can run on the device, so counters, field teams and vehicles keep working when the network is congested or unavailable, with records synchronised later.
Can a fingerprint module be added to a standard model?
Yes. Fingerprint, ID card and NFC modules can be specified on the XH-100, XH-400 and other platforms as an OEM configuration, and the module set is fixed before the housing and mainboard are built, so the device is assembled around the module rather than adapted afterwards.
Is SDK documentation provided?
API or SDK support is provided for the modules installed in your build, so your application can drive the fingerprint sensor, the ID card reader, the NFC reader or the 2D scan engine. Software requirements are captured at the feasibility stage and the documentation set is agreed with the quotation.
Can a biometric tablet read passports?
Yes, in two ways. The optical route reads the two or three-line machine-readable zone; the chip route uses the NFC reader following ICAO 9303, with basic access control and PACE on newer documents. Chip coverage is not universal, so MRZ reading stays available as a fallback for documents that carry no chip. See how document authentication at the border works.
Is face recognition available, and does it include liveness detection?
Yes. Face recognition with liveness detection is available on tablet models, including the XH-300, at a 0.2–1.5 m working distance with recognition in one second or less and a listed verification accuracy of 99.72% or better. Liveness detection is what rejects a photograph or a mask instead of a face.
Which smart cards and contactless cards can it read?
The 13.56 MHz NFC reader handles ISO/IEC 14443 Type A/B, ISO/IEC 15693, 18092 and MIFARE at a listed reading distance of 2–4 cm, which covers contactless bank cards, transit cards, chip-based identity cards and access badges. Contact cards are not part of the standard module set — send the card type at the specification stage and we will confirm what the build can support.
