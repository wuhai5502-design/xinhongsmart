IP68 and the military-grade build

IP68 is the rating this build carries: dust-tight, and sealed against continuous immersion. Together with the 1.2 m drop test at power-on and a −20 to +60 °C operating range, that is the combination buyers mean when they ask for a military-grade tablet — a device that survives the handling and the weather, not only the office.

One caveat before a tender: the specification lists the test figures, not a MIL-STD-810H report. If the project requires that standard by name, ask for the report with the quotation, because a rating printed on a page and a report attached to a bid are two different things. See the MIL-STD-810H guide for what each test method covers.

Where it fits

Vehicle and field work: at 246 × 148 mm the XH-800 fits a dash mount where a 10-inch tablet does not, and the −20 °C floor covers a cold van or an unheated depot. IP68 covers the wet and dusty end of that range, so rain, wash-down and flour dust are inside the rating rather than a question mark.

Warehouse and stock counts: add the Newland or Zebra engine and the tablet handles receiving and cycle counts. For one-handed picking at height a handheld PDA is still the better tool; 750 g held in one hand for a shift is a different job.

Identity workflows: the optional fingerprint, ID card and iris modules put registration on the same screen as the record. Matching and access rules are implemented by your application, not by the device.

Frequently asked questions

Is the XH-800 waterproof and dustproof?
Yes. The build is IP68: dust-tight, and sealed against continuous immersion rather than only splashes. The drop test is 1.2 m onto a wooden floor with the tablet powered on, and the operating range is −20 to +60 °C. If the tender names MIL-STD-810H, ask for the test report with the quotation — the specification lists the figures, not the standard.
What memory options are there?
4 GB + 64 GB is standard. 6 GB + 128 GB and 8 GB + 128 GB are optional, and the TF slot takes a card up to 512 GB. The memory tier is fixed with the order.
Which scan engines can be fitted?
Newland N1, Newland N2596 or Zebra 4100, as listed in the specification. Which one suits you depends on the symbologies and the working distance, so name the labels you scan and the engine is matched to them.
How long does the battery last?
The sheet lists 10 hours of 1080p video at 70% volume and 70% brightness. That is a playback figure, not a shift figure: cellular use, the scan engine, screen brightness and the application all move it. Ask for a measurement on a unit configured like your build if runtime decides the purchase.
Does it run Google Mobile Services?
GMS is not listed in the specification. If your application depends on Google Play services, raise it before the order so the build and the certification can be scoped together.
