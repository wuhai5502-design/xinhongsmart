How to choose

  • Enrolment on the move — XH-100 is a 400 g handheld with fingerprint, 2D scanning and NFC, suited to door-to-door registration and mobile KYC.
  • One operator, many applicants — XH-400 adds a 10.1-inch screen, IP65 sealing and RJ45 Ethernet for fixed and semi-fixed registration points.
  • Two-sided counter work — the dual-screen terminal shows the operator and the applicant the same workflow, and it reads ID or social security cards directly.
  • Platform requirements in a tender — the dual-screen terminal can ship on Android, HarmonyOS or a domestic Kylin configuration.

Biometric device questions

Which fingerprint standards do the modules meet?

Fingerprint capture is offered as capacitive and optical modules across the range, and the standards a programme has to meet are confirmed against the sensor before the build is fixed. Our article on FAP, FBI and ISO fingerprint standards sets out what each certification means for a deployment.

Can these devices read national ID cards?

Yes. The XH-400 can be built with an ID card reader, and the dual-screen terminal accepts a second and third generation social security card reader as an option. Card reader support depends on the scheme and the country, so send us the card type and we will confirm the module.

What platforms does the dual-screen terminal run?

Android on RK3568 quad-core is the default, with RK3576 and RK3588 octa-core as options. HarmonyOS on RK3576 and a domestic configuration on RK3588 with Kylin are also available, which matters for public sector tenders that specify a domestic platform.

What is the minimum order and lead time for a registration programme?

MOQ is 2 units, a sample takes about 3 days and mass production runs about 25 days. Registration programmes usually also need certification for the destination market, so the compliance scope is agreed before tooling starts — see certification support.
