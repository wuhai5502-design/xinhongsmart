OEM · ODM · MECHANICAL

Industrial Design & Tooling for Rugged Terminals

Appearance changes are cheap; structural changes are not. This page separates the three routes — finishing changes that reuse existing tooling, structural changes that need partial tooling, and a new enclosure that needs full tooling — so you can see where your requirement lands before asking for a quotation. The deciding question is almost always whether the change touches the outside of an existing part or the part itself.

THREE ROUTES

Where your requirement lands

Finishing changes

The tooling stays the same, so the lead time and cost stay close to a standard build.

  • Housing colour and texture
  • Silk-screen printing and your logo
  • Device model name and rating-label marking
  • Packaging, manual and accessory set

Structural changes

Parts change, so there is some tooling work and some verification work after it.

  • Relocated or added buttons and ports
  • A different front bezel or connector cover
  • Mounting features for a dock, kiosk or console
  • Internal headers brought out to a base interface

New enclosure

A full ODM enclosure: more engineering, new tooling, and a longer route to the first sample.

  • A different screen size or overall form factor
  • Housing shaped around a specific installation
  • New thermal and sealing design
  • Tooling built for your product alone
VERIFICATION

What changes when the housing changes

AreaWhy it matters
Ingress protectionThe rating that applies to a platform was verified against that housing, seals and port covers. Change any of them and the rating has to be re-verified rather than carried across
Drop and vibrationA new enclosure redistributes mass and changes how impacts travel through the case, so mechanical testing belongs to the verification stage of the new part
Thermal behaviourSealed enclosures move heat differently; a change can affect sustained performance under load
ClearancesModules such as scan engines, antennas and fingerprint windows need defined windows and clearances, which have to be respected in the new geometry
Packing and shippingA different footprint changes the retail box, the carton quantity and palletisation, all of which are agreed before the first batch ships

None of this makes structural work unattractive — it means the verification cost belongs in the plan instead of arriving as a surprise at acceptance.

SEQUENCE

From sketch to tooled enclosure

FAQ

Industrial design questions

Can the housing colour and logo be changed without new tooling?
Yes. Housing colour, texture, silk-screen printing, the device model name and the marking on the rating label can all change while keeping the existing tooling, which is why branding changes usually stay on the low-cost side of an OEM build.
When does a housing change mean new tooling?
When the change is structural rather than cosmetic: a different button layout, a relocated port, a different screen size or a completely new enclosure. Those changes need new tooling and new verification, moving the project onto the ODM route with additional engineering time and cost.
Does changing the enclosure affect the IP rating?
It can. The ingress protection figure that applies to a platform was verified against that specific housing, so once the enclosure, the seals or the port covers change, the rating has to be re-verified rather than assumed. Build this into the schedule rather than discovering it at acceptance.
Can the device be designed to mount in a kiosk or vehicle dock?
Yes. Some platforms expose internal USB, TTL serial, GPIO and SPI headers inside the housing, which can be brought out to a dock or base interface. Whether that covers your installation depends on what needs to connect, and it is settled during feasibility.
